Close your eyes and think of something very, very small. Think of a single, tiny grain of sand on a vast, quiet beach. Now, think of something even smaller than that. Imagine a tiny, shimmering dot that is so small you can barely see it. A long, long time ago, everything in the whole universe was tucked inside a tiny spot just like that. All the stars you see at night were inside it. All the planets and all the trees were in there too. It was very, very small, and it was also very, very hot. It was like a little seed waiting to grow into a big garden.

Then, something wonderful happened. That tiny spot began to grow. Some might imagine it made a loud noise like a big pop, but it was actually very quiet. It was a giant, silent stretch. Imagine you have a soft, colorful balloon that is not blown up yet. When you blow air into it, the balloon gets bigger and bigger. The sides of the balloon move away from each other. They make more room inside. This is how the universe began its journey. It stretched out in every direction to make space. It created the space where the sun and the moon would one day rest. It made room for the drifting clouds, the soaring birds, and the deep, blue sea.

This stretching did not stop after it started. The universe is still growing right now. Every single second, it gets a little bit larger. While you are tucked into your warm, soft, cozy bed, the sky is growing. The stars are slowly moving further apart. It is like the universe is taking a very long, very slow breath. It is always making more room for everything inside of it.

Right now, in our story, the universe is still very new. It is still very, very hot and very bright. Everything is moving so fast that it looks like a glowing soup. It is too hot for anything to stay still. But as the universe keeps stretching, it will start to change. It will start to feel a little bit cooler. It will start to slow down and rest. Everything is getting ready for the next part of the story.

As the universe continued to grow, it remained very, very hot. Imagine a big pot of soup bubbling on a stove. Inside this soup, tiny little pieces were whirling around. They were moving so fast that they could not catch each other. It was like a game of tag where everyone was moving too fast to stop. Because it was so hot, the pieces could not stay still or hold onto each other.

But as the universe stretched out, it started to cool down. Think of a cup of cocoa that is too hot to touch. If you wait a little while, it gets cooler and calmer. The universe did the same thing. As it cooled, the tiny pieces started to slow down. They were not zooming as much anymore. They began to bump into each other gently.

When they bumped into each other, they stuck together. It was like magic building blocks fitting into place. These little pieces made the very first things in the whole universe. They were the simplest pieces possible. One kind was very light. The other kind was just a little bit heavier. These simple building blocks were the only things in the universe for a long, long time.

Before this happened, the universe was very crowded. It was like a thick, white, heavy fog on a rainy morning. You could not see through it. Light was trying to move, but it kept bumping into the busy pieces. It was stuck in the fog. But once the pieces snapped together into those first building blocks, the fog began to go away.

Suddenly, there was more room to move. The light could finally travel across the big, wide space. The universe was no longer a dark, thick soup. It was becoming clear, open, and vast. Everything was quiet and peaceful. The first pieces were floating in the dark, cool space. They were resting and waiting for the next part of the story to begin.

In the quiet that followed, the universe became filled with soft clouds of gas. It was dark and still. But there was a gentle pulling feeling everywhere. Imagine a giant, soft hug that pulls things closer and closer. This pull started to gather the clouds into big, round balls. As the balls grew bigger, they got heavier. They started to get very warm.

The middle of these balls got so hot that they began to glow. This was the birth of the very first stars. They twinkled like nightlights in the dark space. These stars were doing something very special inside. They were like little kitchens. They took the simple pieces of the universe and squeezed them together. They squeezed them so hard that they made brand new things.

In these star kitchens, the universe made the ingredients for everything we see today. It made the stuff that makes up hard rocks. It made the air we breathe. It made the water we drink. These stars worked for a long, long time to make all these gifts. When the stars grew very old, they puffed these new ingredients out into the dark. They spread them far and wide like seeds in a garden.

Those seeds eventually came together to make our Earth. They made the tall, green trees and the bright, blooming flowers. And most importantly, they made you. The very same pieces that were once inside a glowing star are now inside your body. The strong parts of your bones and the tiny pieces in your blood were made in the heart of a star. You are made of stardust.

As you breathe in and out, you are breathing pieces of the great, wide universe. You belong here, among the stars. You are part of the big story that started a long, long time ago. Close your eyes now and feel how quiet the world is. You are safe. You are home. You are a part of everything.
